3. SafeInstall

SafeInstall helps you put together a pre-start check and a Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) on your phone. Please read this section carefully.

Your legal duties stay with you

Under work health and safety laws (in Victoria, occupational health and safety laws), the duty to manage risk, to prepare a SWMS for high-risk construction work, to consult workers, and to make sure the work is done the way the SWMS says belongs to the business conducting the work, and in some cases to the principal contractor and the workers themselves. Those duties can't be handed to a website. SafeInstall is a tool you can use to help meet them, the way you might use a template or a form.

The example content is a starting point

  • The hazards, controls and checklists are general examples for common security installation work. They aren't written for your site and may be incomplete, out of date, or wrong for your job.
  • Before you sign, send or rely on any SafeInstall document, you must review it, remove anything that doesn't apply, add hazards and controls specific to the site and task, and consult the workers who will use it.
  • Producing a document with SafeInstall doesn't mean the document, or your work, complies with the law. We don't check, approve, store or keep records of what you produce.
  • Requirements differ between states and territories and change over time. Guidance shown for your chosen state is general only. Check current requirements with your regulator.
  • If conditions change on site, or a control can't be followed, stop work and reassess. Don't keep going because a document says the job is safe.

Don't overstate what it is

Don't present a SafeInstall document as prepared, checked, approved or endorsed by InstallHub, SafeInstall, a regulator, or anyone other than the person and business who signed it. The document is yours, and so is responsibility for its content.

Sending documents

When you use Send, you're asking us to email the document to the addresses you enter. Only send to people who expect it, and only include other people's names and details (such as workers listed on a SWMS) where you're entitled to. Keep your own copies. 4. TechMate and Ask TechMate AI

  • TechMate guides are written in plain language from manufacturers' documentation and link back to it. Products and firmware change, and guides can contain mistakes. Guides marked Draft haven't yet been checked on site by a working tech.
  • Ask TechMate AI is an automated assistant. Its answers can be wrong, incomplete or out of date, even when they sound confident. Answers that come from the wider web, not TechMate's guides, are labelled. Treat every AI answer as a pointer, not an instruction.
  • Always confirm against the official manual before you change programming, default a panel, or do anything that could wipe settings or footage, disable monitoring, or leave a site unprotected.
  • Work within your licence. Leave 240 V work to licensed electricians.
  • Only work on systems you're authorised to access. Don't use TechMate to get into a system without the owner's authority. We don't publish factory default codes.
  • Don't type customer names, addresses, alarm codes or passwords into Ask TechMate AI.
